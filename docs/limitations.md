# Limitations

The [write-up](write-up.md#limits-and-what-id-do-next) names four limits, each with what I'd do next; this page holds the full list. Two more next steps, beyond those four: hiding the screen from accessibility services on Android 14 and newer, which needs native code, alongside the bank's server-side fraud signals; and closing the accessibility gaps under [Languages and text size](#languages-and-text-size), so that spoken announcements and the native navigation bar use the picked language, a newly shown error scrolls into view at large text, and TalkBack's focus returns to what opened a sheet.

## What I could not prove

Nothing here ran on a physical phone: every device check used an Android emulator, on Google's own image at API level 37, or an iOS 26.5 simulator, and some of this module's security only a real phone can prove.

- **Device-only security is not device-verified.** On the iOS simulator the keys are software, the signing library draws its own prompt, there is no Secure Enclave, and the keychain ignores the setting that should demand authentication. On the Android emulator the keystore is software too, so the hardware guarantee needs a real phone. Release builds of the demo and production variants ran on the emulator and the simulator; physical devices, Android 16 and older, and the next Expo SDK remain untested.
- **No key attestation.** Nothing yet proves to the bank that a signing key lives in a phone's secure hardware: Android key attestation and Apple App Attest are production steps, and the Secure Enclave and manufacturers' Android builds are unverified on real devices.
- **The phone check's own answer.** No device showed what the signing library's root and jailbreak check returns on the emulator or the simulator: the demo build skips the check, and the production build, with no bank to reach, never gets past Home to an approval. The refusal was seen only through the Demo menu's stand-in, and the route tests cover the library's answers.
- **VoiceOver only on the simulator.** There, VoiceOver's own log names the voice that read each element and announcement, but a test driver cannot move its cursor: what it reads first as each screen opens, and what it announces, were checked, and swiping through a screen was not. No screen-reader check in this project uses a real iPhone.
- **The blur in the iOS switcher.** On the simulator the blur covered the app on every screen walked, the Face ID prompt showed over a blurred app, and at the library's default strength the figures were illegible even in a full-resolution screenshot; a real iPhone's switcher is unchecked. Once the app is in the background, iOS keeps a snapshot of it for the switcher, and where capture is blocked that snapshot is black, not blurred. After a Face ID approval, the result page opens while the app is still under the blur, so on iOS its entrance plays unseen and the person sees the blur lift instead.
- **The capture block on iOS.** The simulator takes no screenshot or screen recording of its own: its capture runs on the Mac, outside the phone, and is never blocked. The block was seen only through two stand-ins, the switcher's stored snapshot and the UI-test runner's screenshot, both black on the blocked screens walked and not on a receipt. Whether a screenshot, a recording or AirPlay mirroring on a real iPhone comes out blank is unproven.
- **The capture block on Android.** On the emulator, the block came out blank in screenshots, in screen recordings and in the recent-apps thumbnail on every screen walked, production's Home and, under the demo build's Screenshots blocked pick, the screens production cannot reach; a recording from review into a receipt stayed blank until the receipt had settled. It stayed on after Back and reopening and after a change of text size, when Android gives the app a new window ([008](decisions/008-hardening.md)). A screenshot saves a black picture, but Android's own notice says the app detected the screenshot, not that it refused it: the library registers for screenshot events at every launch, and stopping that needs native code. Only Google's emulator image was available, so how manufacturers' builds of Android honour the block is unverified.
- **The touch guard** is read from the code, not seen working: the emulator has no overlay app to test it with.
- **A locked-out fingerprint on Android.** With the bank's PIN locked and the fingerprint locked out after too many attempts, pressing Transfer on the emulator created the transfer and opened Android's own request for the phone's PIN to recover fingerprints, not the PIN-locked sheet: the signing library's Android side reports no lockout before a fingerprint is asked for. That transfer is never approved and lapses, so no money moves, and once fingerprints are recovered the PIN-locked sheet shows. I accepted this as an Android gap. No simulator can lock Face ID out, so on iOS only the route tests check that the press creates nothing and shows the PIN-locked sheet.
- **Haptics and motion.** On the emulator, the phone's vibration log showed the result page firing three distinct effects, success when money moved, a warning while it was not confirmed, an error when none moved, once each time that changed, and none on the way through the steps or after a cancelled prompt; the simulator has no haptics, so iOS's were never seen. With reduced motion on, at launch or mid-session, screens fade on Android and cross-fade on iOS, the result page's content and its tick stay still, and the Demo menu opens and History's chevron turns at once; turned off again, the platform's own transitions return. The result page's entrance and the tick's settle showed on the simulator and the emulator. The feel of the haptics, and how the motion runs on a real phone, are unchecked: an emulator has no vibration motor, and its frame rate says nothing about a phone's.
- **What the device passes walked, and what they did not.** Earlier device runs walked the approval paths (face, fingerprint and PIN, the lockouts, the bank's PIN lock, an approval key the bank no longer knows, a changed fingerprint, No biometrics), the result page's notices, the hold reasons under both screen readers, the recipient step with contacts, and History, all in development builds and in English. The release-build passes did not walk those again, beyond approving transfers by fingerprint and by Face ID, a face that did not match, and the Android lockout, on the way: they walked what came after, the capture block, the switcher blur, the rooted-phone refusal, the three languages, haptics and motion, and the accessibility bar at 200% text under TalkBack and VoiceOver. Not walked in any release build:
  - production past Home, which has no bank to reach, so its approval, its phone check and its receipts never ran on a device;
  - the result of an approval that expired, on either platform, and of one stopped by the fraud check, on iOS;
  - TalkBack through the Demo menu and the PIN sheet; at 200% text on Android, the session-ended, not-found and crash screens, History empty or failing, and Home during maintenance; at 200% on iOS, the not-found and crash screens, History empty or failing, Home during maintenance, the contacts-refused notice, review's notices other than the first-transfer warning and the refusal, and the result page's unexpected state;
  - the contacts chooser in Malay and Chinese; Voice Control, which the simulator lacks; on either platform, the app following a change of the phone's own language;
  - a crash on a receipt, since no build ships a way to crash a screen; and Back during the phone check, or during the question the press asks the phone about its sensor once the bank has locked the PIN, or a check that never answers, since the demo build answers its stand-in at once and production never reaches review; the route tests cover all of these;
  - the app's own words for a face or finger not recognised, which neither the emulator nor the simulator shows: both keep their own prompt open after a non-match, so the route tests alone cover them;
  - fixes made after the passes, which the route tests alone check: History's rows at large text, room to scroll clear of the offline notice, a press stopped wherever review is left, the PIN lock read before a press can create a transfer, with "Use PIN instead" hidden until then, and no transfer created while the PIN is locked and the phone has locked face and fingerprint after too many attempts; and on iOS, the capture block sent to the phone on every block rather than only the first, which came with the fix that lets each request reach the app's current window, and only Android walked;
  - the latest fixes, which no device has walked yet: the ID field's keyboard following the ID type, the result's heading read first under VoiceOver, nothing beneath the session-ended screen speaking, a heading read once Sign in again lifts that screen, no failure that was only the session's end said in History or on a transfer's details once it lifts, nothing unexpected said on review for a transfer the session's end kept from being created, and only the session's end reported for it or for recent recipients it failed, Try again after too many name checks offered only once the wait is over, a second refusal after that Try again announced again, and a short amount led back to the amount step, the result page opening straight from review with no other screen of the transfer between them, and an earlier transfer reported as still being checked only once the launch has read it, Try again offered on the result page, in History and on a transfer's details only while the phone reports a connection, and the bank sheet's Try again asking the bank even while it reports none, all of which the route tests check; the bank picker's matches and a screen's next control kept clear of the keyboard, and a sheet's title read first as it opens, which component tests check against stand-in screens and layout; Home's language button kept whole at the largest text sizes, a pressed card fading only as far as a pressed button does, the balance's failure card padded as the loaded balance is, and the balance on Home and the amount step kept whole at the largest text sizes, which nothing checks; and in the demo build, the strip kept from a screen reader while a sheet is open, which a route test checks, and the strip held to a few lines at the largest text sizes and without a blank band under it on Android, which nothing checks.
- **Home's warning after the result page is left early.** Home and review now ask the bank again, every 15 seconds or so while their warning shows, about the newest transfer still being checked, and an approval's answer is recorded even when its result page never shows, as when Android's Back skips it, so Home says how it ended or warns while it is still being checked. Both fixes came after the device passes, and only the route tests check them.
- **History's speed on iOS.** The [re-render counts](history-re-render-counts.md) were taken on Android; iOS was not re-measured, since a release build's logs never reached iOS's log in the spike, and the iOS counts it did take matched Android's. No count says anything about frame rate.

## What the bank and the host app own

- **Proving the phone's integrity.** The rooted-phone check runs on the phone, so it can be fooled, and it can refuse a genuine customer whose phone misreports. The refusal takes the place of the approval prompt and offers no appeal inside this module, and no way to approve anyway. The real control is the bank verifying the phone on its servers, through Google Play Integrity and Apple App Attest; this module names that step and builds none of it. Separately, Bank Negara Malaysia's Risk Management in Technology policy, in Appendix 11, paragraph 3, expects the bank to detect and end hijacked sessions promptly, and to warn a customer when a risky app raises their risk; that sits on the bank's side too.
- **Apps that read the screen.** Blocking screenshots does not stop a malicious accessibility service or a remote-control app from reading the screen, a common route for Android banking scams in Malaysia. Android 14 can hide an app's views from all but genuine accessibility tools, but that needs native code I chose not to add yet; with the bank's server-side fraud signals, it is the production answer.
- **Unblocked moments.** The capture block is off during the splash screen before the app's code loads, which shows no data. On the way into a receipt it stays on for 600 milliseconds after the receipt's route opens, an allowance meant to outlast the transition, so the screen being left, such as the review with its recipient and amount, is not captured as it slides away; on a phone slow enough that the transition outlasts it, the transition's last frames would be. On the emulator, a recording from review into a receipt stayed blank until the receipt had settled. Leaving a receipt puts the block back before the next screen is drawn. And when the bank ends the session while a receipt is open, the session-ended screen that covers it can be captured as the receipt could: the block follows the route beneath, and that screen shows no account data, at most the reference of a transfer still being checked.
- **The host app.** Sign-in, device pairing, PIN setup and reset, the biometrics setting and session handling belong to the banking app this module runs inside.
- **Someone holding the phone and the PIN** can approve through the PIN path. What acts against them is the bank's: every transfer and every biometric change is notified, and the account can be locked. Three wrong PINs lock the PIN at the bank, which stops someone guessing it, not someone who knows it.

## Languages and text size

- Malay and Chinese are machine translations, used as they came and reviewed by no one. A glossary fixes the money phrases and the retry words, but the translations' overall quality is unknown.
- On iOS, the app tells VoiceOver which language each text and control is in, and on the simulator VoiceOver read them in its Malay and Chinese voices. Three things carry no language. The screen titles and the back buttons drawn by the native navigation bar carry none on either platform: a header of the app's own would lose the bar's own animation. Nor do spoken announcements, since React Native's call that makes one takes no language: on the simulator, a Malay result was announced in the English voice, and a Chinese refusal in Japanese and Chinese voices by turns. Giving an announcement its language needs native code, which this module does not add. Android has no such setting at all, so TalkBack reads Malay and Chinese in the phone's own speech language, as it did on the emulator, and nothing here closes that. The words Android adds itself follow the phone's language too, not the app's: its own back button's "Navigate up", and "busy" on a button that is waiting.
- The Chinese is simplified. The app matches the phone's language on its language code alone, so a phone set to traditional Chinese gets the simplified text too.
- Malay and Chinese were walked on the emulator and the simulator through every screen of a transfer, History and the Demo menu, dates included. On both, a pick applied at once and was kept across a relaunch, the first screen after it already in the picked language with no English frame first.
- The accessibility bar was walked at 200% text: in English, on the emulator at font scale 2 and on the simulator at the accessibility text size nearest 200%, and in Malay, the longest language, on the simulator's review, PIN sheet and result page. Apart from the back buttons below, every control met 48 dp on Android and 44 pt on iOS, with a role and a name. Two failures found there were fixed after the walk, and only the route tests check the fixes: History broke names and words mid-word, and the floating offline notice covered content that could not scroll clear of it. What remains:
  - The native screen titles keep their size whatever the text size, on both platforms; the screens with headers of their own scale them up to twice their size, past which a title's longest word would break. Home's language button in that bar grows to one and a half times its size, and no further.
  - The navigation bar's back button on the recipient step and History measures 30 by 30 dp on Android, under the 48 dp the app's own controls meet. iOS's own back chevron is 43 by 37 pt, a system control.
  - At 200% on iOS, an error or notice that appears below the visible part of a screen is not scrolled into view: the amount's over-limit error while the keyboard is up, and the notice that a picked contact has no Malaysian mobile number. Screen readers still announce both. A 16-character reference is wider than its row and breaks mid-token, since no layout can keep it whole at that size.
  - When the text size shrinks with the app open, Home's warning about an earlier transfer, and the Demo strip, keep their large height until the app restarts; screens opened afterwards lay out normally.
  - Under TalkBack, closing a sheet sends focus to the top of the screen rather than back to what opened it; picking an ID type, or folding or unfolding a History day, says nothing until focus comes back to it; and on Home, a balance that failed to load is announced again each time a sheet opens or closes.
- Typing an amount gives no haptic tick: the amount is typed on the phone's own keyboard, which already clicks or buzzes on each key on most phones.
- Opened with no connection and nothing loaded yet, History says "Couldn't load your transfers." and a transfer's details say "Couldn't load this transfer." though nothing has failed: the read is only waiting for a connection, and loads by itself once the phone has one.
- The bank sheet can say "You're offline. Sending is paused until Ryt can be reached." twice. When the phone loses its connection with the sheet open, the sheet and the app's own notice beneath it each say it; and since the sheet asks for its list even offline, a request that reaches the bank meanwhile has the list asked for again, and the sheet says it again. The route tests show both; whether a screen reader on a device says the second over the first is unchecked.
