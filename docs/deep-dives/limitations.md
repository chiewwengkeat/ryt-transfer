# Limitations

The [write-up](../write-up.md#limits-and-what-id-do-next) names four limits, each with what I'd do next; this page holds the full list. Two more next steps, beyond those four: hiding the screen from accessibility services on Android 14 and newer, which needs native code, alongside the bank's server-side fraud signals; and closing the accessibility gaps under [Languages and text size](#languages-and-text-size), so that spoken announcements and the native navigation bar use the picked language, a newly shown error scrolls into view at large text, and TalkBack's focus returns to what opened a sheet.

## What I could not prove

Only the screens' layout was checked on a physical phone, one Android phone that also recorded the demo video: every other device check used an Android emulator, on Google's own image at API level 37, or an iOS 26.5 simulator, and some of this module's security only a real phone can prove.

- **Device-only security is not device-verified.** On the iOS simulator the keys are software, the signing library draws its own prompt, there is no Secure Enclave, and the keychain ignores the setting that should demand authentication. On the Android emulator the keystore is software too, so the hardware guarantee needs a real phone. Release builds of the demo and production variants ran on the emulator and the simulator; physical devices, Android 16 and older, and the next Expo SDK remain untested.
- **No key attestation.** Nothing yet proves to the bank that a signing key lives in a phone's secure hardware: Android key attestation and Apple App Attest are production steps, and the Secure Enclave and manufacturers' Android builds are unverified on real devices.
- **The phone check's own answer.** No device showed what the signing library's root and jailbreak check returns on the emulator or the simulator: the demo build skips the check, and the production build, with no bank to reach, never gets past Home to an approval. The refusal was seen only through the Demo menu's stand-in, and the route tests cover the library's answers.
- **VoiceOver only on the simulator.** There, VoiceOver's own log names the voice that read each element and announcement, but a test driver cannot move its cursor: what it reads first as each screen opens, and what it announces, were checked, and swiping through a screen was not. No screen-reader check in this project uses a real iPhone.
- **The blur in the iOS switcher.** On the simulator the blur covered the app on every screen walked, the Face ID prompt showed over a blurred app, and at the library's default strength the figures were illegible even in a full-resolution screenshot; a real iPhone's switcher is unchecked. Once the app is in the background, iOS keeps a snapshot of it for the switcher, and where capture is blocked that snapshot is black, not blurred. After a Face ID approval, the result page opens while the app is still under the blur, so on iOS its entrance plays unseen and the person sees the blur lift instead.
- **The capture block on iOS.** The simulator takes no screenshot or screen recording of its own: its capture runs on the Mac, outside the phone, and is never blocked. The block was seen only through two stand-ins, the switcher's stored snapshot and the UI-test runner's screenshot, both black on the blocked screens walked and not on a receipt. Whether a screenshot, a recording or AirPlay mirroring on a real iPhone comes out blank is unproven.
- **The capture block on Android.** On the emulator, the block came out blank in screenshots, in screen recordings and in the recent-apps thumbnail on every screen walked, production's Home and, under the demo build's Screenshots blocked pick, the screens production cannot reach; a recording from review into a receipt stayed blank until the receipt had settled. It stayed on after Back and reopening and after a change of text size, when Android gives the app a new window ([008](design-decisions/008-hardening.md)). A screenshot saves a black picture, but Android's own notice says the app detected the screenshot, not that it refused it: the library registers for screenshot events at every launch, and stopping that needs native code. Only Google's emulator image was available, so how manufacturers' builds of Android honour the block is unverified.
- **The touch guard** is read from the code, not seen working: the emulator has no overlay app to test it with.
- **A locked-out fingerprint on Android.** With the bank's PIN locked and the fingerprint locked out after too many attempts, pressing Transfer on the emulator created the transfer and opened Android's own request for the phone's PIN to recover fingerprints, not the PIN-locked sheet: the signing library's Android side reports no lockout before a fingerprint is asked for. That transfer is never approved and lapses, so no money moves, and once fingerprints are recovered the PIN-locked sheet shows. I accepted this as an Android gap. No simulator can lock Face ID out, so on iOS only the route tests check that the press creates nothing and shows the PIN-locked sheet.
- **Haptics and motion.** On the emulator, the phone's vibration log showed the result page firing three distinct effects, success when money moved, a warning while it was not confirmed, an error when none moved, once each time that changed, and none on the way through the steps or after a cancelled prompt; the simulator has no haptics, so iOS's were never seen. With reduced motion on, at launch or mid-session, screens fade on Android and cross-fade on iOS, the result page's content and its tick stay still, and the Demo menu opens and History's chevron turns at once; turned off again, the platform's own transitions return. The result page's entrance and the tick's settle showed on the simulator and the emulator. The feel of the haptics, and how the motion runs on a real phone, are unchecked: an emulator has no vibration motor, and its frame rate says nothing about a phone's.
- **A blank moment after a PIN approval on Android.** As the PIN sheet closes on an approval, Android can show it over plain grey, or a near-white screen, for 0.08 to 0.19 seconds before the result page draws, with review gone from beneath it: 4 of 12 approvals did so on one emulator. A change meant to keep review drawn beneath the closing sheet removed it in 8 approvals on another emulator, but not on this one, and the cause is not settled. iOS showed none.
- **On Android, the PIN sheet's content drops** about 28 dp as the sixth digit is entered, so Approve moves just after the last digit; iOS, which shows the same line, was not checked.
- **What the device passes walked, and what they did not.** Earlier device runs walked the approval paths (face, fingerprint and PIN, the lockouts, the bank's PIN lock, an approval key the bank no longer knows, a changed fingerprint, face or fingerprint unavailable), the result page's notices, the hold reasons under both screen readers, the recipient step with contacts, and History, all in development builds and in English. The release-build passes did not walk those again, beyond approving transfers by fingerprint and by Face ID, a face that did not match, and the Android lockout, on the way: they walked what came after, the capture block, the switcher blur, the rooted-phone refusal, the three languages, haptics and motion, and the accessibility bar at 200% text under TalkBack and VoiceOver. Since then, a production release build walked the bank sheet saying why it has no list and offering Try again, and a balance that fails to load, which is no longer said again each time the app asks for it again, under TalkBack and VoiceOver. Demo release builds walked, on both platforms, a pressed card fading only as far as a pressed button does, the balance's failure card padded as the loaded balance is, the balance card holding one height at the default text size while it loads, once loaded and when it says the balance couldn't be loaded, so nothing below moves between them, and shorter during maintenance, when it shows the balance alone, the balance on Home kept whole at the largest text sizes, a busy button such as review's Transfer keeping its own colour so its spinner shows, and History keeping its words whole at 200% text; and on Android, Try again offered on the result page, in History and on a transfer's details only while the phone reports a connection, the bank sheet's Try again asking the bank even while it reports none, a return to the front with none asking it nothing, walked with no screen reader on, the status bar's icons drawn dark, and, under TalkBack, History saying nothing of a first page that failed only because the session ended, once Sign in again lifts the session-ended screen, and the language sheet's title read first as it opens, with nothing from Home or the Demo strip spoken beneath it. Two fixes made after those walks, which route tests pin, keep the maintenance card compact, and Transfer's reason for waiting in view, while the app asks for the limits again on returning to the front. Not walked in any release build:
  - production past the amount step: with no bank to reach it never gets to review, so its approval, its phone check and its receipts never ran on a device;
  - the result of an approval that expired, on either platform, and of one stopped by the fraud check, on iOS;
  - TalkBack through the Demo menu and the PIN sheet; at 200% text on Android, the session-ended, not-found and crash screens, History empty or failing, and Home during maintenance; at 200% on iOS, the not-found and crash screens, History empty or failing, Home during maintenance, the contacts-refused notice, review's notices other than the first-transfer warning and the refusal, and the result page's unexpected state;
  - the contacts chooser in Malay and Chinese; Voice Control, which the simulator lacks; on either platform, the app following a change of the phone's own language;
  - a crash on a receipt, since no build ships a way to crash a screen; and Back during the phone check, or during the question the press asks the phone about its sensor once the bank has locked the PIN, or a check that never answers, since the demo build answers its stand-in at once and production never reaches review; the route tests cover all of these;
  - the app's own words for a face or finger not recognised, which neither the emulator nor the simulator shows: both keep their own prompt open after a non-match, so the route tests alone cover them;
  - fixes that only development builds walked: on iOS, the ID field's keyboard following the ID type, and the result's heading read first by VoiceOver; the app's own words held beneath the session-ended screen, and a heading read once Sign in again lifts that screen, which the route tests check for iOS alone; Try again after too many name checks offered only once the wait is over, a second refusal after that Try again announced again, and a short amount led back to the amount step; the result page opening straight from review, which shows it is still sending until then, with no other screen of the transfer between them; an earlier transfer reported as still being checked only once the launch has read it; the bank picker's matches and a screen's next control kept clear of the keyboard; a sheet's title read first as it opens, on Android for every sheet but the language sheet, while on iOS VoiceOver reads Close first and then moves to the title; Home's language button kept whole at the largest text sizes; and the Demo strip kept from VoiceOver while a sheet is open, and from TalkBack under every sheet but the language sheet, held to a few lines at the largest text sizes, and without a blank band under it on Android;
  - fixes only the route tests check: a press stopped wherever review is left, and the PIN lock read before a press can create a transfer, with "Use PIN instead" hidden until then; nothing unexpected said on review for a transfer the session's end kept from being created, and only the session's end reported for it or for recent recipients it failed; no failure that was only the session's end said for History's later pages or its refresh, or on a transfer's details, once Sign in again lifts the session-ended screen; the bank sheet saying nothing again on that return to the front with no connection, which no screen reader has listened for; and on iOS, where the simulator always reports a connection, what Try again does with no connection on the result page, in History, on a transfer's details and in the bank sheet, and a return to the front with none, as well as no transfer created while the PIN is locked and the phone has locked face and fingerprint after too many attempts, and the capture block sent to the phone on every block rather than only the first, which came with the fix that lets each request reach the app's current window, and only Android walked;
  - a fix nothing checks and no device has walked: the balance on the amount step kept whole at the largest text sizes.
- **Home's warning after the result page is left early.** Home and review now ask the bank again, every 15 seconds or so while their warning shows, about the newest transfer still being checked, and an approval's answer is recorded even when its result page never shows, as when Android's Back skips it, so Home says how it ended or warns while it is still being checked. Both came after the release-build passes, and only development builds walked them: the re-check on both platforms, and the answer recorded when Android's Back skips the result page.
- **History's speed on iOS.** The [re-render counts](history-re-render-counts.md) were taken on Android; iOS was not re-measured, since a release build's logs never reached iOS's log in the spike, and the iOS counts it did take matched Android's. No count says anything about frame rate.

## What the bank and the host app own

- **Proving the phone's integrity.** The rooted-phone check runs on the phone, so it can be fooled, and it can refuse a genuine customer whose phone misreports. The refusal takes the place of the approval prompt and offers no appeal inside this module, and no way to approve anyway. The real control is the bank verifying the phone on its servers, through Google Play Integrity and Apple App Attest; this module names that step and builds none of it. Separately, Bank Negara Malaysia's Risk Management in Technology policy, in Appendix 11, paragraph 3, expects the bank to detect and end hijacked sessions promptly, and to warn a customer when a risky app raises their risk; that sits on the bank's side too.
- **Apps that read the screen.** Blocking screenshots does not stop a malicious accessibility service or a remote-control app from reading the screen, a common route for Android banking scams in Malaysia. Android 14 can hide an app's views from all but genuine accessibility tools, but that needs native code I chose not to add yet; with the bank's server-side fraud signals, it is the production answer.
- **Unblocked moments.** The capture block is off during the splash screen before the app's code loads, which shows no data. On the way into a receipt it stays on for 600 milliseconds after the receipt's route opens, an allowance meant to outlast the transition, so the screen being left, such as the review with its recipient and amount, is not captured as it slides away; on a phone slow enough that the transition outlasts it, the transition's last frames would be. On the emulator, a recording from review into a receipt stayed blank until the receipt had settled. Leaving a receipt puts the block back before the next screen is drawn. And when the bank ends the session while a receipt is open, the session-ended screen that covers it can be captured as the receipt could: the block follows the route beneath, and that screen shows no account data, at most the reference of a transfer still being checked.
- **An approval the bank says expired.** The result page reads it as no money moved without checking whether the money left the account; a transfer's details page checks, and so does the result page for a transfer that failed. That holds only while the bank never debits a transfer whose approval expired, which its contract must guarantee.
- **A transfer the bank says it cannot find.** The phone then stops checking it and drops the record it kept, though whether money moved is not confirmed. When that answer reaches the result page, the page says once that the transfer can't be found and to check Transfer history before sending again, and nothing warns about it after Done or a relaunch; when it reaches Home's re-check or the check at launch, Home just stops warning about it.
- **The host app.** Sign-in, device pairing, PIN setup and reset, the biometrics setting and session handling belong to the banking app this module runs inside.
- **A second customer on one phone.** The app keeps only the signed-in customer's records of transfers still being checked: at launch, and at a sign-in after the session ended, it discards any other customer's. On a phone two customers share, a transfer still being checked for one loses its warning once the other signs in.
- **Someone holding the phone and the PIN** can approve through the PIN path. What acts against them is the bank's: every transfer and every biometric change is notified, and the account can be locked. Three wrong PINs lock the PIN at the bank, which stops someone guessing it, not someone who knows it.

## Languages and text size

- Malay and Chinese are machine translations, used as they came and reviewed by no one. A glossary fixes the money phrases and the retry words, but the translations' overall quality is unknown.
- On iOS, the app tells VoiceOver which language each text and control is in, and on the simulator VoiceOver read them in its Malay and Chinese voices. Three things carry no language. The screen titles and the back buttons drawn by the native navigation bar carry none on either platform: a header of the app's own would lose the bar's own animation. Nor do spoken announcements, since React Native's call that makes one takes no language: on the simulator, a Malay result was announced in the English voice, and a Chinese refusal in Japanese and Chinese voices by turns. Giving an announcement its language needs native code, which this module does not add. Android has no such setting at all, so TalkBack reads Malay and Chinese in the phone's own speech language, as it did on the emulator, and nothing here closes that. The words Android adds itself follow the phone's language too, not the app's: its own back button's "Navigate up", and "busy" on a button that is waiting.
- The Chinese is simplified. The app matches the phone's language on its language code alone, so a phone set to traditional Chinese gets the simplified text too.
- Malay and Chinese were walked on the emulator and the simulator through every screen of a transfer, History and the Demo menu, dates included. On both, a pick applied at once and was kept across a relaunch, the first screen after it already in the picked language with no English frame first.
- The accessibility bar was walked at 200% text: in English, on the emulator at font scale 2 and on the simulator at the accessibility text size nearest 200%, and in Malay, the longest language, on the simulator's review, PIN sheet and result page. Apart from the back buttons below, every control met 48 dp on Android and 44 pt on iOS, with a role and a name. It found two failures. The floating offline notice covered content that could not scroll clear of it, fixed since, and only the route tests check the fix. History broke names and words mid-word; since then, a row's amount and a day's total move below the text when a row lacks room for both, and a demo release build kept every word whole at 200% on the emulator, set 360 dp and 411 dp wide, and on the simulator. What remains:
  - The native screen titles keep their size whatever the text size, on both platforms; the screens with headers of their own scale them up to twice their size, past which a title's longest word would break. Home's language button in that bar grows to one and a half times its size, and no further.
  - The navigation bar's back button on the recipient step and History measures 30 by 30 dp on Android, under the 48 dp the app's own controls meet. iOS's own back chevron is 43 by 37 pt, a system control.
  - At 200% on iOS, an error or notice that appears below the visible part of a screen is not scrolled into view: the amount's over-limit error while the keyboard is up, and the notice that a picked contact has no Malaysian mobile number. Screen readers still announce both. A 16-character reference is wider than its row and breaks mid-token, since no layout can keep it whole at that size.
  - At the largest iOS text size on an iPhone SE, review's two buttons, Transfer and Use PIN instead, take about 70% of the screen in Malay, leaving a strip of the review above them to scroll.
  - At the two largest iOS accessibility text sizes, a word or number wider than a History row's text column still breaks inside it. On a 402 pt simulator, "Unsuccessful" and a long one-word name, "SUBRAMANIAM", broke at both sizes; on a 360 dp emulator at 200% the same name stays whole. Computed from React Native's own layout code and the system font, and not seen beyond that phone: "Unsuccessful" breaks on any iPhone narrower than about 410 pt at the second largest and on every iPhone at the largest, the Malay "keselamatan" of a held transfer's status below about 389 and 425 pt, and at the largest, on the smallest iPhones such as the SE, "Successful", "Processing" and a five-figure amount's digits too.
  - When the text size shrinks with the app open, Home's warning about an earlier transfer, and the Demo strip, keep their large height until the app restarts; screens opened afterwards lay out normally.
  - In the demo build, at the largest iOS text sizes the Demo pill is cut off at the screen edge, and at 200% the Demo strip pushes the app down about 64 px.
  - On iOS, changing the text size while History is open draws the larger text clipped at the right edge until the screen is opened again.
  - On Android, when a link opened the app, changing the phone's text size with the app open takes it back to the linked screen: on the emulator, History or a transfer's details came back in place of Home, three times in three.
  - Under TalkBack, closing a sheet sends focus to the top of the screen rather than back to what opened it, and picking an ID type, or folding or unfolding a History day, says nothing until focus comes back to it.
  - On the emulator, TalkBack's touch exploration found nothing in a sheet's body, only its title and Close: held there, the bank sheet's words and Try again and the language sheet's rows said nothing, though swiping reached each of them and the same hold found a button on the screen beneath once the sheet closed. The holds were sent through the emulator rather than by a finger, and why they find nothing is not known.
  - In the demo build, TalkBack moves from the result page's heading to the Demo strip about two seconds after the heading takes focus, and on review it goes to the strip first: review, unlike the result page, sends no screen reader to its heading.
  - Under VoiceOver, review's refusal after too many name checks is cut off at its sixth word and then read again whole: VoiceOver is already reading the refusal it has focused when the app's announcement of it begins. TalkBack says it once. When the amount step's loading spinner sat outside the balance card, VoiceOver also read "Your balance couldn't be loaded." there a second time, about four seconds after the app announced it, moving onto the notice from the spinner it had focused, where TalkBack said it once; the spinner now sits inside the card, and whether VoiceOver still reads the notice twice is unchecked.
- Typing an amount gives no haptic tick: the amount is typed on the phone's own keyboard, which already clicks or buzzes on each key on most phones.
- Opened with no connection and nothing loaded yet, History says "Couldn't load your transfers." and a transfer's details say "Couldn't load this transfer." though nothing has failed: the read is only waiting for a connection, and loads by itself once the phone has one.
- The bank sheet can say "You're offline. Sending is paused until Ryt can be reached." twice. When the phone loses its connection with the sheet open, the sheet and the app's own notice beneath it each say it; and since the sheet asks for its list even offline, a request that reaches the bank meanwhile has the list asked for again, and the sheet says it again. Both showed in Jest runs made to look for them, but no committed test checks either; whether a screen reader on a device says the second over the first is unchecked.
